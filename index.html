The problem

A swamp cooler in Miami is a $400 humidifier.

Evaporative coolers are miracles in the desert and furniture in the humidity - and the box will not tell you which one you are buying. The physics is one number: your wet-bulb depression. Swampmath computes it from your temperature and humidity, then shows the air the cooler can actually deliver.

Check your air

What it computes

Wet bulb
The coldest temperature evaporation can reach in your air (Stull approximation, the standard published formula).
The depression
Outside temp minus wet bulb. That gap is your entire cooling budget - no cooler beats it.
Honest supply air
Real coolers close 65-90% of the gap (the published effectiveness range for direct evaporative media). See the exact supply temperature and the humidity it adds.
The verdict
11°C+ of depression: buy with confidence. 6-11°C: it takes the edge off, nothing more. Under 6°C: save your money.

Why the box lies by omission

  • "Cools up to 20 degrees!" - in Phoenix at 10% humidity. The same unit in Houston at 70% has a physics ceiling of about 4°C before its own effectiveness losses.
  • Cooling and humidifying are the same process here. The cooler lowers temperature only by raising humidity. In a muggy climate it makes the room feel worse, not better.
  • Effectiveness is not on the sticker. Basic pads close ~65% of the depression, rigid media ~80%. Two coolers in the same store can differ by a third of their real output.

Who it is for

  • Desert dwellers checking whether a basic pad unit is enough

Estimation tool: wet bulb via the Stull (2011) approximation (published for -20 to 50°C, 5-99% RH), dew point via August-Roche-Magnus, sea-level pressure assumed. Room temperature never reaches supply temperature - infiltration, walls and people add heat. Verdict bands are common guidance, not standards.
